## ACT 1: NIGHTTIME FIELDS OF HWASEONG & THIRTY-THREE YEARS IN THE MIST

**[BGM]:** Somber cello melodies, chilling wind whipping across barren winter fields, and rhythmic rain evoking an oppressive atmosphere of mourning.

**HOST** *(deep tone, opening with historical solemnity)*:
In the late 1980s, across the rural expanse of Hwaseong in Gyeonggi Province, not far from Seoul, an unprecedented pall of grief and dread settled over South Korea.
Beginning in the autumn of 1986 and stretching through the spring of 1991, a string of ten horrific murders unfolded along quiet rice paddies and secluded farm trails.
The victims were local women of diverse ages, ambushed in the dark as they returned home from work or crossed solitary rural paths.
The uncanny repetition of the attacks and their sheer brutality transformed Hwaseong into the most terrified region in modern Korean history.
Whenever twilight descended, no woman dared venture beyond her doorway, and whispered folklore regarding the color red became an enduring nightmare.
It became the nation’s most infamous serial murder case, serving as the real-life inspiration for the cinematic masterpiece Memories of Murder.

**[SFX]:** Distant patrol car siren wailing through the rainy night, accompanied by scattered, frantic footsteps on unpaved dirt.

**HOST** *(recounting the unprecedented scale of the investigation)*:
To hunt down that invisible predatory phantom, the South Korean government launched an investigative mobilization of unprecedented proportions.
More than two million police officers and military personnel were deployed to comb every irrigation ditch, thicket, and alleyway across Gyeonggi Province.
Over twenty-one thousand persons of interest were summoned, interrogated, and fingerprinted over years of relentless operations.
Investigative teams endured immense public scrutiny, with dozens of veteran detectives dedicating their youth and entire careers to finding a trace of the killer.
Several officers suffered physical exhaustion and mental collapse, with some tragically taking their own lives under the agonizing weight of the prolonged deadlock.
Yet despite monumental human effort, the perpetrator always seemed one step ahead, vanishing without a trace into the dense fog.
In 2006, South Korea’s fifteen-year statute of limitations for the Hwaseong murders officially expired, leaving behind an open wound devoid of resolution.

**[SFX]:** Heavy, oppressive ticking of a clock, paired with the gentle rustle of aged cold-case archive folders.

**HOST** *(the grief spanning more than three decades)*:
For over three decades, the Hwaseong case stood as the most agonizing failure of Korean jurisprudence and an indelible scar upon collective memory.
Victims’ families endured daily heartbreak, never knowing who had extinguished the lives of their cherished daughters, mothers, and sisters.
Local residents lived under a cloud of mutual suspicion, wondering whether the monster still walked freely among them.
Justice seemed permanently entombed beneath layers of dust and the aging of the original investigative team.
Yet dedicated law enforcement professionals never granted themselves the luxury of abandoning the search for truth.

**ORDINARY PERSON** *(N1: 1980s Hwaseong resident, middle-aged female voice, shaken and emotional)*:
For those of us living in Hwaseong during the 1980s, the memory of those icy winter nights remains a waking nightmare.
Whenever we heard sirens or shouting out in the fields, our hearts sank because we knew another neighbor had fallen.
My parents never allowed my sisters or me outside after dark, and every farmhouse bolted its wooden doors before six in the evening.
Rumors circulated that the killer only ambushed women dressed in red on rainy nights, so for years no one dared wear anything red.
An atmosphere of deep suspicion settled everywhere; men in the village looked at each other with guarded eyes, terrified that a familiar face was the beast.
For thirty-three long years, we lived with bitter sorrow, believing the killer would carry his secrets to the grave without answering for his deeds.
Yet deep in our hearts, we clung to the hope that heaven was watching and that the truth would eventually step into the light.

**HOST** *(the 2019 breakthrough of forensic DNA technology)*:
That fragile hope was finally realized through the extraordinary leaps of modern forensic science.
In the autumn of 2019, the Gyeonggi Nambu Provincial Police Agency reopened the archived evidence lockers, submitting preserved biological samples to the National Forensic Service.
Using cutting-edge DNA amplification technology, forensic scientists succeeded in extracting microscopic genetic material from the garments of victims from cases five, seven, and nine.
That profile was immediately cross-referenced against the national offender database, yielding an electrifying, perfect match.
The killer was no phantom of the dark, but a convicted murderer serving a life sentence at Busan Prison named Lee Choon-jae.
A new psychological chess match was about to commence within the penitentiary walls, shattering thirty-three years of evasion beneath the shadows.

**INVESTIGATOR** *(Gyeonggi Provincial Police Agency Investigator, middle-aged male voice, calm and resolute)*:
The instant our screens confirmed the DNA match with Lee Choon-jae, our entire task force stood frozen in overwhelming emotion.
This man was fifty-six years old, arrested and sentenced to life imprisonment back in 1994 for the murder of his sister-in-law in Cheongju.
Throughout twenty-five years at Busan Prison, Lee Choon-jae had cultivated the facade of a model inmate, quiet, skilled in crafts, and even eligible for parole review.
However, our greatest legal dilemma was that the statute of limitations on every Hwaseong murder had expired in 2006, meaning we could not charge him anew.
Our sole purpose entering Busan Prison was to dismantle this killer’s psychological defenses to restore truth and dignity to the victims and their families.

**HOST** *(closing Act 1, transitioning to the duel in Busan)*:
An elite unit of investigators alongside South Korea’s foremost criminal profilers set out immediately for Busan Prison.
Yet what awaited them inside the interview room was neither stubborn stonewalling nor belated tears of remorse.
It was the bone-chilling nonchalance of a serial killer proud of the horrors he had wrought.
