## ACT 1: THE TERROR ON THE STREETS OF DUSSELDORF AND COLLECTIVE HYSTERIA

**HOST** *(slowly, in a warm and reflective documentary tone)*:
In the late nineteen twenties, Germany under the Weimar Republic was struggling beneath the unhealed wounds of the First World War.
A catastrophic economic crisis, runaway unemployment, and the collapse of traditional social values cast a suffocating veil of existential anxiety over society.
People lived in a profound state of uncertainty, where mutual trust between individuals had grown exceedingly fragile.
In the bustling industrial city of Dusseldorf along the Rhine, the daily routines of working-class citizens were already fraught with hardship.
Yet, within that fragile and exhausted environment, an unseen phantom emerged, unleashing unprecedented terror upon the urban landscape.
Dark street corners and deserted public parks after sunset abruptly became the hunting grounds of a faceless predator.
Victims spanned across all ages and backgrounds, from innocent young schoolgirls to mature adult women, and even able-bodied adult men.
The violence struck entirely at random, devoid of any conventional criminal motive such as robbery or personal vendetta.
That very unpredictability created a treacherous “Social Blindspot” that paralyzed the entire metropolitan community.
Citizens began viewing one another with intense suspicion, terrified that any lone stranger emerging from the evening fog might be the bloodthirsty beast.
A wave of collective hysteria erupted like an uncontrollable wildfire, grinding public life across the thriving industrial region to a virtual standstill.
One of the darkest and most psychologically complex cases in global judicial history began amid just such a collective nightmare.

He introduced a revolutionary investigative methodology: rather than hunting blindly for physical traces, police had to decode the psychological profile of the predator.
Gennat recognized that to trap this phantom, they needed to comprehend the inner workings of a calculating human mind.
He immediately established a centralized homicide task force, deploying standardized file cards that meticulously cataloged the timing and geography of every incident.

Viewed through the lens of psychoanalysis, the collective hysteria in Dusseldorf in nineteen twenty-nine illustrates Carl Gustav Jung’s archetypal “Shadow”.
When a civilization endures severe systemic trauma and economic ruin, the collective unconscious activates its deeply buried primal dread.
The widespread panic surrounding this invisible killer was a psychological projection of the populace's own unacknowledged helplessness and societal despair.

## ACT 2: THE PERFECT MASK AND COGNITIVE COMPARTMENTALIZATION

**HOST** *(gravely leading the narrative)*:
Picture a middle-aged gentleman of polite demeanor, neatly groomed hair, and a soft-spoken, respectful manner toward everyone he encountered.
He was an industrious factory laborer who was never late for work and strictly adhered to every company regulation.
In the eyes of neighbors at the apartment building on number seventy-one Mettmanner Street, he was a model of modesty and quiet decency.
He lived with his gentle wife in a humble apartment kept so spotless that not a speck of dust could be found.
He routinely helped elderly neighbors carry heavy grocery baskets up the stairwell and offered warm greetings to young children in the courtyard.
Yet, behind that placid domestic facade lay a horrifying reality that defied human comprehension.
That courteous gentleman was Peter Kurten, the very individual terrorizing the entire urban population of western Germany.
He possessed no outward physical deformities or bizarre mannerisms that might arouse suspicion from passersby.
His mask of normality was reinforced by an unflinching composure across every mundane routine of daily existence.

Subsequent case logs compiled by Ernst Gennat recorded the profound astonishment of detectives when examining the suspect's domestic living space.
The perpetrator had constructed an impeccably structured everyday life showing no external signs of mental breakdown or social deviance.
He had married Auguste Scharf in nineteen twenty-three, a woman who had survived severe hardships and yearned for stability amid national turmoil.
Throughout seven years of marriage, Kurten played the role of an attentive, devoted, and respectful husband.
He returned home punctually, handed his complete paycheck to his wife to manage, and escorted her in clean attire on weekend strolls.
His protective disguise functioned so flawlessly that even the woman sharing his bed for seven years suspected nothing of his double life.
Behavioral crime scene profiling revealed the pinnacle of “Modus Operandi” combined with an extreme psychological “Compartmentalization Signature”.
He possessed the terrifying ability to switch mental states instantly, transforming from a mild factory worker into a ruthless predator without hesitation.

This pathological defense mechanism allows an individual to isolate atrocious behaviors entirely from their functional everyday consciousness.
At home or at his workplace, his social persona functioned with immaculate efficiency, securing respect and social validation.
However, that psychological mask was not formed by accident; it was forged through severe childhood trauma and unrelenting domestic violence.
Kurten grew up in extreme poverty as one of thirteen children, terrorized by an alcoholic father who subjected the family to brutal physical abuse.
He witnessed horrifying acts of degradation within the cramped confines of a single room, where basic human dignity was systematically crushed.
To survive his father's cruelty, the child learned to dissociate from emotional empathy, constructing a distorted fantasy of omnipotent dominance.

Beginning with cruel acts against domestic animals in his youth, a perverse link between inflicting harm and asserting self-worth took root in his psyche.
Extended prison sentences for petty theft during his early adulthood did nothing to rehabilitate him, serving only to refine his deceitful camouflage.
Behind prison bars, he stewed in cold resentment toward society, transforming childhood humiliation into a systematic desire for retribution.

**HOST** *(delving into the offender's narcissistic core)*:
What was most alarming about Peter Kurten was not merely his assaults under the cover of night, but how he savored the aftermath of his violence.
Following each major incident, he did not flee or hide like an ordinary criminal terrified of capture.

## ACT 3: SURVIVAL IN THE FOREST AND THE FATEFUL LETTER

**[BGM]:** Minimalist piano notes strike solitary and cold, accompanied by the gentle sound of rainfall outside a window.

**HOST** *(gravely marking the turning point)*:
On May 14, 1930, after more than a year of terror, an apparently minor encounter completely altered the course of the investigation.
A twenty-year-old domestic servant named Maria Budlick arrived in Dusseldorf from Cologne in search of household employment.
Stepping onto the platform of the massive central railway station, the destitute young woman felt utterly lost in an unfamiliar city.
She carried all her worldly belongings in a worn cloth bag, burdened by the urgent necessity of finding work to support her impoverished family.
It was precisely at that railway terminal that Peter Kurten presented himself as a benevolent benefactor.
With impeccably courteous manners and a reassuring smile, he approached the young woman, offering to help her secure respectable lodging.
Suspecting nothing sinister behind the well-dressed gentleman, Maria Budlick accepted his offer with relief.
He informed her that the women's hostel did not open until evening, inviting her to his apartment for a warm meal while waiting.
His calculated display of paternal kindness disarmed every natural instinct of caution in the vulnerable young traveler.

**[SFX]:** Footsteps crunching on dry pine needles in the dark, night wind whistling through dense woodland.

**HOST** *(shifting to a breathless tone, recreating the encounter in the forest)*:
After a simple meal at his apartment on seventy-one Mettmanner Street, he offered to escort her to the lodging house on the edge of town.
However, rather than heading toward the illuminated city streets, he steered the young woman into the deep darkness of the Grafenberg Forest.
As the final streetlamps vanished in the distance, the benevolent mask of the respectable gentleman dropped away.
The atmosphere turned deadly as he forced her off the trail into a secluded thicket beneath the dense canopy of trees.
Facing imminent death in the wilderness, survival instinct prompted the young woman to execute a remarkably astute psychological strategy.
She sensed the terrifying emptiness behind the predator's eyes, realizing that physical resistance would mean immediate death.

**ORDINARY PERSON** *(N1: Maria Budlick - Surviving victim, youthful female voice trembling yet resolute)*:
I have never encountered eyes as terrifying as his in my entire life.
They were not the eyes of a normal human being, but a cold, bottomless void completely devoid of empathy.
When I realized I was trapped entirely alone in the woods with an attacker, I understood that screaming would get me killed immediately.

I feigned absolute submission, making myself appear as fragile, harmless, and insignificant as possible.

Summoning every ounce of composure I had left, I looked directly into his eyes and spoke with absolute simplicity: “I remember nothing at all. I am a complete stranger to this city, and every single street looks identical to me”.
He stared into my face for a long time searching for deceit, but I kept my expression completely vacant and overwhelmed.

**PSYCHOLOGIST** *(explaining tactical compliance and victim survival response)*:
Maria Budlick’s conduct represents a classic demonstration of tactical compliance or appeasement within trauma psychology.
When confronting an assailant with overwhelming physical advantage and weapon lethality, reflexive fight-or-flight responses prove fatal.
Maria instinctively deployed an optimal survival strategy: de-escalating the threat, humanizing her vulnerability, and eliminating the perpetrator's paranoia.
Peter Kurten chose to spare her life not out of mercy, but because his colossal narcissism was successfully deceived.
He genuinely believed that an impoverished girl from out of town, disoriented in the dark, posed zero threat of retracing his path.
That supreme arrogance was the fatal blindspot that ultimately precipitated his downfall.

**HOST** *(recounting the chain of events following the encounter)*:

She secured temporary shelter with an employer and wrote a tearful letter to a friend in her home village, detailing the ordeal and describing the apartment.
However, a remarkable twist of fate intervened: the envelope was misaddressed.
The unintended recipient opened the letter and immediately recognized the startling similarities to published police notices.
The document was delivered directly to the desk of Ernst Gennat at Dusseldorf Criminal Police headquarters.
Gennat instantly recognized that they had finally obtained the golden link sought fruitlessly for over fifteen months.
A discreet, high-priority manhunt was mobilized that very night to locate the building described in the letter.

In that singular moment, Auguste Scharf’s entire reality shattered into pieces.

Recognizing that escape was impossible, Kurten noted that municipal authorities had posted an enormous financial bounty for information leading to the killer's capture.

Her disclosure was not an act of opportunism; it was an act of moral survival to salvage her humanity from total corruption.
She chose the social ostracism of being an infamous killer's wife over remaining a passive shield for ongoing slaughter.

**[BGM]:** Warm, solemn piano chords resonate gently, reflecting the quiet relief of a society emerging from an overwhelming nightmare.

**HOST** *(in an authoritative, historic documentary tone)*:
Following his apprehension, Peter Kurten showed none of the panic or frantic denial typical of common felons.
He sat in his cell at Dusseldorf prison with an unsettling serenity, answering every question with cold, clinical precision.
Over the ensuing year, he became the primary research subject of Dr. Karl Berg, Director of the Dusseldorf Institute of Forensic Medicine.
Hundreds of hours of psychiatric examinations were documented, forming the foundation of the landmark treatise *Der Sadist* — one of the world's first comprehensive psychiatric studies of a serial sexual killer.
And it was within those extensive sessions that the true nature of Kurten's supposed “remorse” was scientifically dissected.
He treated the clinical interview sessions as a platform to display his pathology to an audience of prominent scientists.

**PSYCHOLOGIST** *(analyzing instrumental remorse and predatory narcissism)*:
Dr. Karl Berg's clinical findings reached an unambiguous conclusion: Peter Kurten was entirely devoid of genuine moral remorse.
His cooperative demeanor before investigators was merely a manifestation of “Instrumental Remorse” designed to serve his monumental narcissism.
He recounted every brutal detail not out of atonement, but to re-experience the sensation of absolute power he once wielded over helpless victims.
He treated the interrogation cell as a grand stage, where he remained the captivating center of attention for leading forensic authorities.
This absolute emotional vacancy was epitomized in his final question to Dr. Berg moments before stepping up to the guillotine:
He inquired whether he would be able to hear the sound of his own blood gushing from his neck after the blade fell, describing it as the ultimate pleasure to end all pleasures.
That chilling statement remains indelible proof that his personality was entirely severed from the basic moral conscience of humanity.
He regretted nothing about the suffering inflicted upon victims and their loved ones, mourning only that his personal theater of domination had reached its finale.

**INVESTIGATOR** *(summarizing the trial and legal legacy)*:
In April nineteen thirty-one, the trial of Peter Kurten unfolded before an international press gallery.
The court sentenced him to nine death sentences for nine conclusively proven homicides.
On July 2, 1931, the execution was carried out via guillotine at Klingelputz prison in Cologne.
The closure of the case ended a dark chapter, while bequeathing monumental advances to the evolution of modern forensic science.
Ernst Gennat’s innovations in centralized crime index cards, mobile forensics laboratories, and behavioral crime profiling established foundational standards used by international law enforcement to this day.
Justice encompassed not merely the punishment of a single offender, but the construction of scientific barriers to safeguard human life in generations to come.
